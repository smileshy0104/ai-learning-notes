<div class="tag">Lecture outline · Introduction（slides 1–22）</div>

<p>Set the computation aside for a moment. This part answers three questions: what is this course about, where did today's language models come from, and where does the Transformer fit in?</p>
<h2 id="part-2">1. What the course is for: understand the mechanics and the usage</h2>
<p>The slides state two goals: understand how a Transformer works and how it relates to large language models, and understand how large language models are trained and how they are used in applications. You can read the course as a path from “how the model computes” to “how the model is used in real systems”.</p>
<div class="flow" aria-label="Course path"><span class="chip">Architecture: how information flows</span><span class="arrow">→</span><span class="chip">Training: how parameters are learned</span><span class="arrow">→</span><span class="chip">Applications: how tasks get done</span></div>
<p>When an assistant answers a question, you can ask separately: how does it read the input, how were those abilities learned, and when does it need to search or call a tool? Those questions belong to architecture, training and applications. This lecture builds the architecture foundation.</p>
<h2 id="part-3">2. What background is assumed?</h2>
<p>The slides address researchers, practitioners building personal projects, and learners who want to understand AI. The stated prerequisites are machine-learning basics and linear algebra. Fill in the gaps as you go:</p>
<div class="grid"><div class="layer"><strong>Linear algebra</strong><br>Vector: a list of numbers<br>Matrix: numbers arranged in rows and columns<br>Dot product: multiply matching entries and sum<br>Matrix product: many dot products at once</div><div class="layer"><strong>Machine learning</strong><br>Parameter: a number adjusted during training<br>Prediction: what the model outputs for an input<br>Loss: how far the prediction is from the target<br>Training: adjusting parameters using the loss</div></div>
<p class="small muted">These are orientation sketches. When a specific computation comes up, we give definitions and worked examples; you do not need to memorise every acronym now.</p>
<h2 id="part-4">3. Four eras: from task-specific NLP to agentic systems</h2>
<p>NLP stands for Natural Language Processing: getting computers to handle human language. Click each era to see the problem it solved. The timeline follows how the slides are organised, and the eras overlap.</p>
<div class="flow" aria-label="Timeline used in the course"><a class="chip" href="#era-task">2010s · task-specific</a><span class="arrow">→</span><a class="chip" href="#era-transformer">2017 · Transformer</a><span class="arrow">→</span><a class="chip" href="#era-chat">2022 · chat</a><span class="arrow">→</span><a class="chip" href="#era-agent">the present, per the slides · agents</a></div>
<details id="era-task" open><summary>2010s: different tasks often meant different models</summary><p>The common approach was to train or adapt a dedicated model per task. Examples:</p><ul><li><strong>Sentiment analysis:</strong> input “this restaurant is great”, output “positive”.</li><li><strong>Machine translation:</strong> input an English sentence, output the French equivalent.</li><li><strong>Named-entity recognition:</strong> in “Zhang San lives in Beijing”, mark the person “Zhang San” and the place “Beijing”.</li></ul><div class="flow"><span class="chip">review → sentiment model → label</span><span class="chip">English → translation model → French</span><span class="chip">sentence → tagging model → tags</span></div><p>RNNs were one important sequence-modelling approach at the time. Later sections cover how they pass information through hidden states, and why long-range dependencies and sequential computation are hard. This is a simplified main line, not a claim that only RNNs existed.</p></details>
<details id="era-transformer" open><summary>2017: the Transformer provides a scalable architectural base</summary><p>“Attention Is All You Need” introduced the Transformer. The original task was machine translation: an encoder and a decoder, with self-attention as the key mechanism, where each position can gather information directly from the positions it is allowed to see.</p><p>“Scalable” here means, roughly: with a suitable training recipe, adding data, parameters and compute can keep improving capability. The matrix form of Transformer computation also suits parallel execution during training. More compute does not guarantee every ability improves; data quality and training design matter just as much.</p><div class="note"><strong>The Transformer is an architecture; an LLM is a large language model.</strong> Think of the architecture as the design of how computation modules connect. Training learns the parameters. Modern large language models usually use Transformer variants, but an architecture diagram alone does not give you a model you can chat with.</div></details>
<details id="era-chat" open><summary>2022: ChatGPT brought conversational interaction to a wide audience</summary><p>The slides treat the ChatGPT release as a turning point in product and interaction. Users could ask in natural language, add constraints, request rewrites and complete many tasks within one conversation.</p><div class="flow"><span class="chip">Summarise this text</span><span class="chip">Translate it into English</span><span class="chip">Explain this code</span><span class="arrow">→</span><span class="chip">many tasks through one conversation</span></div><p>Many tasks can be expressed through a single language-model interface. That depends on scale, training data and training methods together, and chat products may also connect extra models and tools. The Transformer architecture alone does not explain all conversational ability.</p></details>
<details id="era-agent" open><summary>The agent era: from suggesting fixes to calling tools and continuing from the result</summary><p>Take “fix an error” as an example: a chat assistant can explain the error and suggest a change; an agent system with the right tools and permissions can also read files, edit code, run checks and keep adjusting from what it sees.</p><div class="flow" aria-label="Simplified agent loop"><span class="chip">receive a goal</span><span class="arrow">→</span><span class="chip">model picks the next step</span><span class="arrow">→</span><span class="chip">system runs the tool</span><span class="arrow">→</span><span class="chip">result returns to the model</span></div><p>Here “agent” can be read as a system made of a model plus tools, state management and an execution loop. Tools run as external programs; the model decides what to do next from the results it gets. Being able to call a tool is not the same as completing the task correctly: capability still depends on the tools, the context, permissions and verification.</p><p class="small muted">The agent loop is a supplementary sketch for understanding the slides; this lecture does not implement agents in detail.</p></details>
<h2 id="part-5">4. Separating names that are often confused</h2>
<div class="table-wrap"><table><thead><tr><th>Name</th><th>What it is</th><th>Plain-language reading</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Transformer</td><td>A neural-network architecture</td><td>The design of the internal computation modules</td></tr><tr><td>LLM</td><td>A large language model</td><td>A trained model that can process and generate language</td></tr><tr><td>ChatGPT</td><td>A conversational product</td><td>The entry point where users interact with a model and its features</td></tr><tr><td>AI Agent</td><td>A system that executes steps towards a goal</td><td>A model combined with tools, state and an execution loop</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<p>These concepts describe an architecture, a model, a product and a system. Keeping the distinction stops “I learned the attention formula” from being mistaken for an explanation of how a full AI assistant works.</p>

<details><summary>Check your understanding: are ChatGPT, Transformer and Agent the same thing?</summary><p>They describe different layers. Transformer describes the model’s architecture; ChatGPT is a conversational product; Agent describes a system that executes steps towards a goal. A product can use a Transformer-based model and also offer agentic features with tools.</p></details>
<details><summary>Check your understanding: why start a Transformers course with a 2017 translation architecture?</summary><p>It gives one clean, complete example: the source sentence enters the encoder, and the decoder combines the source with what it has already generated to produce the translation. Understand how embeddings, attention and feed-forward networks cooperate first, then learn which components modern models kept, changed or removed.</p></details>
